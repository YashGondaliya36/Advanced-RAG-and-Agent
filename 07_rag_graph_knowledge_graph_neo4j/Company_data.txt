Section 1 — Organizational Overview and Team Composition (as of Q1 2026)
OmniCorp's engineering function is divided into six primary verticals, each operating with nominal autonomy but sharing infrastructure, databases, and enough undocumented inter-service dependencies to make any deployment feel like defusing a bomb in the dark. The verticals are: AI Research (AIR), Core Backend (CBE), Frontend Web (FEW), SEO Optimization (SEO), Cloud Infrastructure (CIF), and Mobile App (MOB). There is also a loosely affiliated Platform Security group that technically reports into CIF but operationally functions as a floating panic button that gets called into incidents by everyone.
The AI Research team is led by Sneha Iyer, who holds the title of Director of AI and reports directly to the VP of Product Engineering. Sneha manages three senior engineers: Karthik Balasubramanian, who owns the Recommendation Engine and its associated Vector Store integrations; Pranav Deodhar, who is the primary on-call for the Embedding Pipeline service and the Graph Analytics DB cluster; and Riya Malhotra, who co-owns the Embedding Pipeline with Pranav but focuses primarily on model evaluation infra and the offline batch jobs that feed into the Vector Store. It should be noted that Riya also has informal ownership over the legacy Spark jobs running on the On-Premises Server Rack A cluster, though this is not reflected in any PagerDuty rotation and has caused significant confusion during incidents (see: The Embedding Degradation Incident, Q3 2025, documented in Section 4).
Core Backend is the largest team and the one most people end up blaming during outages. Raghavendra Pillai (referred to universally as "Raghu") is the Staff Engineer who effectively runs day-to-day architecture decisions, though his formal manager is Divya Krishnamurthy, the Engineering Manager for CBE. Raghu owns the User Auth Service and the Payment Gateway v2, both of which are considered Tier-0 services — meaning any degradation in either cascades catastrophically across the product surface. Under Divya's management sit four additional engineers: Ankit Sharma, who maintains the Payment Gateway v2 alongside Raghu and is the secondary on-call; Meghna Joshi, who owns the internal Admin API and the Notification Dispatch Service; Siddharth Rao, who owns the Customer PostgresDB schema migrations and the ORM layer used by most CBE services; and Tanvir Shaikh, who is nominally responsible for the Legacy Oracle DB and the ETL pipelines that nobody wants to touch but everyone depends on.
Frontend Web is managed by Pooja Nambiar, who reports to Divya Krishnamurthy as well — a reporting structure that made sense two reorgs ago and now mostly serves to create ambiguity about who owns the API contracts between FEW and CBE. The FEW team consists of Pooja herself plus two engineers: Arjun Menon, who owns the Web Client Application and the SEO Metadata Service integration (FEW consumes the SEO Metadata Service but does not own it), and Deepika Srinivasan, who handles component library, performance monitoring, and the internal A/B Testing Tool, which is a janky Node.js service living on a single EC2 instance in AWS us-east-1 that everyone has agreed to migrate "next quarter" for the past six quarters.
The SEO Optimization team is a two-person outfit: Fahim Qureshi, the team lead, and Ishaan Tripathi, the engineer who actually built and maintains the SEO Scraper and the SEO Metadata Service. Fahim reports to the Head of Growth Engineering, not to any of the engineering directors, which makes cross-team escalations interesting. The SEO Scraper is a Python-based crawler that runs on GCP Compute Engine and writes crawl results into the Customer PostgresDB under a dedicated schema. The SEO Metadata Service exposes those results via a REST API consumed by the Frontend Web team. Critically, the SEO Metadata Service also reads from the Session Redis cluster to cache rendered metadata snapshots — a design decision made by Ishaan in early 2024 that is documented nowhere except in a comment in the source code reading "# using redis here bc postgres was too slow lol."
Cloud Infrastructure is managed by Nandita Gokhale, who has the title of Head of Platform Engineering and an on-call rotation that hasn't had a quiet week since the company crossed 10 million users. Her team includes two senior SREs: Vikram Nair, who owns the AWS us-east-1 Kubernetes cluster and the Terraform modules for all EKS provisioning, and Abhishek Pandey, who owns the GCP Compute Engine environment where the SEO Scraper, the Embedding Pipeline, and several AI Research batch jobs run. Nandita herself maintains direct control over the On-Premises Server Rack A environment (located in the Mumbai colocation facility), which houses the Legacy Oracle DB, the archival Hadoop cluster, and Riya Malhotra's aforementioned Spark jobs. The Platform Security subgroup, consisting solely of Omkar Deshpande (who has been trying to get a second headcount approved for eight months), technically reports to Nandita but spends most of his calendar in CBE and AIR incident channels.
The Mobile App team is led by Kavya Reddy, Engineering Manager, who manages two engineers: Suresh Babu, who owns the iOS and Android client codebases, and Nikhil Aggarwal, who owns the Mobile API Gateway — a critical service that sits between the mobile clients and the rest of OmniCorp's backend. The Mobile API Gateway is the single entry point for all mobile traffic and makes synchronous downstream calls to the User Auth Service (owned by Raghu in CBE), the Payment Gateway v2 (co-owned by Raghu and Ankit), the Recommendation Engine (owned by Karthik in AIR), and the Notification Dispatch Service (owned by Meghna in CBE). The Mobile API Gateway maintains its own session validation layer that reads directly from Session Redis — it does not go through the User Auth Service for session checks on hot paths, a performance optimization Nikhil introduced in late 2024 that saved 40ms per request and introduced a category of bugs that took three months to diagnose.

Section 2 — Services, Ownership, and Infrastructure Placement
The Recommendation Engine is deployed on the AWS us-east-1 Kubernetes cluster and is owned by Karthik Balasubramanian. It reads from the Vector Store (a Qdrant cluster also running on AWS us-east-1 EKS) and from the Graph Analytics DB (a Neptune instance on AWS us-east-1 managed by Pranav). The Recommendation Engine exposes a gRPC API consumed by two callers: the Mobile API Gateway and the Web Client Application (via a thin REST wrapper maintained by Arjun Menon). There is no circuit breaker between the Web Client Application and the Recommendation Engine wrapper, which is relevant to Section 4.
The User Auth Service is a Go service deployed on AWS us-east-1 Kubernetes, owned by Raghavendra Pillai. It reads from and writes to the Customer PostgresDB (primary and replica both on AWS us-east-1 RDS) and writes session tokens to Session Redis (an ElastiCache Redis cluster, also us-east-1). The User Auth Service is a dependency of the Payment Gateway v2, the Admin API, the Notification Dispatch Service, and the Mobile API Gateway. The Payment Gateway v2 is deployed on AWS us-east-1 Kubernetes and is co-owned by Raghu and Ankit Sharma. It depends on User Auth Service for token validation on every transaction request, reads account data from Customer PostgresDB, and logs all transaction events to the Legacy Oracle DB via Tanvir's ETL pipeline — a synchronous write that has been flagged as a single point of failure in four separate architecture reviews and has not been addressed because migrating off Oracle requires budget approval from a committee that has not convened since November 2024.
The Notification Dispatch Service, owned by Meghna Joshi, is deployed on AWS us-east-1 Kubernetes. It depends on the User Auth Service to resolve user identity before dispatching, writes notification logs to Customer PostgresDB, and reads user preferences from Session Redis (a decision that mirrors the SEO Metadata Service's Redis usage and was, coincidentally, also made for caching performance reasons with no formal design review). The Admin API, also owned by Meghna, is deployed on the same Kubernetes cluster and is the only service with write access to the Legacy Oracle DB outside of the ETL pipeline — a permission that exists because Tanvir asked for it during a production hotfix in 2023 and nobody revoked it afterward.
The Embedding Pipeline is a Python-based service deployed on GCP Compute Engine, co-owned by Pranav Deodhar and Riya Malhotra. It reads raw content from Customer PostgresDB (via a read replica pointed at GCP via a VPN tunnel that Vikram Nair set up and considers "temporary"), processes embeddings using GPU instances on GCP, and writes results to the Vector Store on AWS us-east-1. The cross-cloud write path goes through a Kafka topic that Nandita Gokhale provisioned but that is not monitored by any current alerting dashboard, a gap that Omkar Deshpande has documented in ticket OMNI-SEC-2241 (open since October 2025). The SEO Scraper runs on GCP Compute Engine, owned by Ishaan Tripathi, writes to Customer PostgresDB, and has a cron-based schedule managed by a Cloud Scheduler job that Abhishek Pandey set up and has since forgotten the credentials for.

Section 3 — Database and Infrastructure Reference
Customer PostgresDB is the most-connected datastore in OmniCorp's estate. It is hosted on AWS RDS us-east-1, with a primary instance and two read replicas. Services with write access to primary: User Auth Service, Payment Gateway v2, Notification Dispatch Service, SEO Scraper (via its GCP-to-RDS VPN path), and Siddharth Rao's migration tooling. Services reading from replica: Recommendation Engine (indirectly, through a reporting view), Embedding Pipeline, Admin API, and the A/B Testing Tool. Schema ownership is nominally with Siddharth, but Tanvir has made undocumented schema changes on four occasions, two of which caused ORM-layer failures in CBE services.
Session Redis is an AWS ElastiCache Redis 7.x cluster in us-east-1, configured with 32GB memory and no eviction policy set (this becomes relevant in Section 4). The services that read from Session Redis are: User Auth Service, Mobile API Gateway, Notification Dispatch Service, and SEO Metadata Service. Only User Auth Service and Mobile API Gateway write to it. There is no Redis Cluster mode; it is a single-node primary with one replica, and the replica is not promoted automatically on failure.
Graph Analytics DB is an AWS Neptune instance owned by Pranav Deodhar. It is consumed exclusively by the Recommendation Engine. The Vector Store is a Qdrant cluster deployed on EKS us-east-1, owned nominally by Karthik but provisioned by Vikram's Terraform modules. The Legacy Oracle DB is hosted on On-Premises Server Rack A in the Mumbai colo, managed by Tanvir, and accessed by Payment Gateway v2 (synchronously via ETL), Admin API, and several untracked internal scripts whose authors have since left the company. AWS us-east-1 Kubernetes (EKS) hosts: User Auth Service, Payment Gateway v2, Mobile API Gateway, Recommendation Engine, Notification Dispatch Service, Admin API, Vector Store, and the A/B Testing Tool (technically, though this is a standalone EC2 that predates the EKS migration). GCP Compute Engine hosts: Embedding Pipeline, SEO Scraper, and assorted AIR batch jobs. On-Premises Server Rack A hosts: Legacy Oracle DB, archival Hadoop, and Riya's Spark batch jobs.

Section 4 — Incident History and Post-Mortem Summaries
The Black Friday Crash of 2025 — November 28, 2025
At 09:47 IST on November 28, 2025, OmniCorp experienced a full checkout flow outage lasting 3 hours and 14 minutes, affecting approximately 2.3 million active sessions. The root cause was a deadlock in the Customer PostgresDB primary instance triggered by a combination of Siddharth Rao's schema migration (OMNI-MAINT-4421, scheduled for 06:00 IST, which ran long) and a traffic spike that caused the Payment Gateway v2 to open an unusually high number of concurrent write connections. Because Payment Gateway v2 depends synchronously on the Legacy Oracle DB ETL write path, and because the Oracle DB was under elevated load from Tanvir's overnight batch reconciliation job (which had not completed on schedule due to a disk I/O issue on Rack A), transaction commits on the payment service began timing out. The timeout cascade propagated upstream: Mobile API Gateway, which calls Payment Gateway v2 synchronously for all checkout flows, began returning 503s to mobile clients. The User Auth Service, also hitting Customer PostgresDB for session writes, began queueing, which caused Session Redis to receive no token refresh writes, causing mobile sessions to expire mid-checkout for users in active flows. PagerDuty fired simultaneously to Raghavendra Pillai (CBE primary), Ankit Sharma (CBE secondary), Nandita Gokhale (CIF primary), and Vikram Nair (CIF secondary). Tanvir Shaikh was manually added to the incident bridge at 10:12 IST by Raghu after it became clear Oracle was involved. Kavya Reddy and Nikhil Aggarwal from Mobile were paged at 10:23 IST when it was confirmed the Mobile API Gateway was the customer-facing blast radius. Resolution involved Siddharth rolling back the migration, Tanvir manually killing the Oracle batch job, and Vikram scaling up the RDS instance class to absorb connection pool demand. Post-mortem action items: 17 items opened, 4 closed as of this writing.
CVE-2026-AuthLeak — February 3, 2026
A security vulnerability was disclosed in the JWT library used by the User Auth Service that allowed token forgery under specific conditions involving algorithm confusion. Omkar Deshpande identified the CVE at 14:30 IST and immediately escalated to Raghavendra Pillai. The vulnerability meant that any service reading from Session Redis could potentially be served a forged session. Affected read-path services: Mobile API Gateway (Nikhil Aggarwal's ownership), SEO Metadata Service (Ishaan Tripathi's ownership), and Notification Dispatch Service (Meghna Joshi). Omkar coordinated a patch deployment across all affected services within 6 hours, requiring simultaneous deploys by Raghu (User Auth Service), Nikhil (Mobile API Gateway), Ishaan (SEO Metadata Service), and Meghna (Notification Dispatch Service). During the patch window, the User Auth Service was in read-only mode for 22 minutes, during which no new sessions could be created, effectively blocking new mobile logins and new web registrations. Arjun Menon (FEW) was pulled in to add a maintenance banner to the Web Client Application. Vikram Nair coordinated the Kubernetes rolling restarts across all services. Post-mortem noted that the SEO Metadata Service's undocumented Redis dependency was discovered only during this incident's blast radius analysis — Pooja Nambiar and Fahim Qureshi were not initially included in the incident bridge because nobody knew SEO Metadata Service read from Session Redis.
The Redis Memory Exhaustion Event — March 19, 2026
At 03:15 IST, PagerDuty fired to Nandita Gokhale and Vikram Nair when Session Redis memory utilization crossed 98%. Investigation revealed that the Mobile API Gateway had introduced a regression in release v4.7.2 (deployed by Nikhil Aggarwal two days prior) that wrote session metadata objects approximately 4x larger than intended due to a serialization bug — a nested user-preferences object was being written in full rather than by reference. With no eviction policy configured, Redis simply filled up. Once memory was exhausted, all write operations to Session Redis began failing. The User Auth Service could no longer persist new session tokens, causing all new login attempts across web and mobile to fail. The Mobile API Gateway's own hot-path session reads began failing as existing keys could not be updated, causing mid-session authentication failures for logged-in users. The Notification Dispatch Service, which reads user preferences from Session Redis, began returning null-preference responses, causing a flood of default-template notifications that bypassed user opt-out settings — a secondary incident in its own right, escalated separately by Meghna Joshi at 04:45 IST. Kavya Reddy was paged at 03:30 IST. Nikhil Aggarwal was paged at 03:31 IST. Siddharth Rao was consulted at 04:00 IST to verify no PostgresDB writes were being dropped as a fallback. Riya Malhotra's Spark jobs on Rack A, which normally run overnight, had a downstream dependency on a Redis-cached configuration flag read by the Embedding Pipeline at startup; with Redis unavailable, the Embedding Pipeline failed to initialize, causing a gap in the Vector Store update cycle that the Recommendation Engine surfaced as stale recommendations for approximately 19 hours after Redis was restored. Resolution: Vikram flushed volatile keys, Nikhil rolled back v4.7.2, and Nandita implemented a maxmemory-policy of allkeys-lru as an emergency configuration change. Permanent eviction policy and memory alerting at 70% threshold added as action items. Karthik Balasubramanian filed a follow-up ticket (OMNI-AIR-889) to decouple the Embedding Pipeline startup from Redis, which remains open.
